# Luxury: image direction and AI prompts

## Intended feeling

Immersive and attentive, with a strong sense of material and place. The image should make someone imagine being there or handling the object.

Controlled directional light, believable reflections, carefully managed highlights, rich midtones, natural material variation. Choose a convincing viewpoint. Use atmosphere to reveal the subject, not to conceal vague product detail.

This guide is original art direction derived from the system's principles. It does not ask an image model to copy a reference site's layout, logo, or proprietary imagery. The [reference review](references.md) explains what was actually observed.

## Build a prompt from decisions

Use this structure, replacing each bracketed field with a concrete creative decision:

> [Specific place or object], [viewpoint and lens character], [controlled light source], [accurate material properties], [selected palette], [compositional quiet area], refined photographic realism, [ratio], no text or branding.

Describe the asset, not an entire finished webpage. Name the subject, intended page role, viewpoint, light, materials, palette, negative space, crop, and exclusions. Request one image per role. HTML owns headlines, body text, prices, navigation, labels, and controls.

## Complete sample prompts

These prompts are for fictional projects and concept assets. They are ready to adapt to a real brief; no output files have been generated by this documentation change.

### Hero / 16:9

> Architectural photograph of a fictional lakeside guest house living room at blue hour, broad window framing still water, charcoal wool seating, pale stone floor and brushed metal details, one warm reading lamp creating a believable pool of light, balanced exposure retaining exterior and interior detail, spacious eye-level composition with the main seating group on the right and calm wall area on the left, 16:9 landscape, tactile realistic materials, no people, no text, no logo, no exaggerated palace styling.

### Material detail / 4:5

> Close still-life photograph of a hand-finished silver vessel beside a single green branch, brushed surface with subtle tool marks and soft controlled reflections, cool gray backdrop, a narrow side light defining the rim, rich shadow detail rather than crushed blacks, asymmetrical museum-quality composition, 4:5 portrait, no jewelry branding, no lettering, no glitter effects.

### Atmosphere / 3:2

> Photographic concept of a small evening dining room after the first guests arrive, linen, dark timber, low amber lamps and a few crystal glasses with restrained highlights, two adult guests seen in relaxed conversation at a distance, candid rather than posed, warm atmosphere with clear separation of materials, 3:2 landscape, realistic perspective, no logos, no text, no extreme lens flare.

## Negative direction

Avoid: Generic marble-and-gold rooms, over-polished skin, implausibly perfect interiors, blown metallic highlights, excessive bokeh, artificial fog, supercars used as status shorthand, and fantasy features added to a real offering.

Do not merely append “premium,” “beautiful,” or “award-winning.” Replace those words with visible decisions about scale, lighting, material, and composition. If the subject is wrong, revise the subject before adding visual effects.

## Crop, theme, and series consistency

Create a wide scene and a portrait companion from the same brief. Do not crop away the detail that establishes the quality of the place or object. Keep 15% safe space around important handles, rims, and architectural edges.

A botanical series can use olive greens and daylight; a hospitality series can use warmer lamps. Lock lighting, material treatment, and palette within a series. Use authorized source imagery for real venues and products; label speculative renders as concepts.

Lock the light direction, lens/viewpoint family, color treatment, and material language for a series. Vary the distance and subject detail, not the entire art direction. Review both the full-size image and a small catalogue thumbnail. Do not invert photography for dark mode; use deliberate crops or compatible transparent edges. Check cutouts on both the light and dark surfaces for halos and lost shadows.

## Handoff to JabKit

Choose each image's role and aspect ratio before generating. Keep the original high-quality output, then create web-sized derivatives with explicit width and height and appropriate responsive sources. Use a meaningful alt description for informative imagery and empty alt for purely decorative assets. Keep a text fallback when media fails.

Store component media locally under the hosted `/assets/...` convention. Follow [preview and asset documentation](../../docs/previews.md); the current vendor command handles supported remote sources and is not a general AI-image importer. Record provenance in the format supported by the repository rather than inventing a remote URL for a generated file. Do not hotlink reference-site assets in components or mocks.

## Art-direction acceptance

The image must serve its section, preserve a recognizable subject at mobile size, leave the required quiet area, and belong to the same series as its neighbors. Reject attractive images with implausible materials, broken object geometry, unintentional text, inconsistent shadows, or a crop that obstructs the action. The page should still communicate the offer if the image is unavailable.
