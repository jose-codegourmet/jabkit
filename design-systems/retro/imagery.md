# Retro: image direction and AI prompts

## Intended feeling

Personal, handled, and lightly playful. The visitor should recognize a material memory without feeling trapped in a costume party.

Paper fibers, subtle edge wear, soft contact shadows, matte plastic, direct but gentle light. For collage, use actual physical overlap and a consistent overhead view. For device renders, retain a consistent three-quarter camera, material roughness, and light direction across the whole set.

This guide is original art direction derived from the system's principles. It does not ask an image model to copy a reference site's layout, logo, or proprietary imagery. The [reference review](references.md) explains what was actually observed.

## Build a prompt from decisions

Use this structure, replacing each bracketed field with a concrete creative decision:

> [Object from the chosen material family], [coherent period cues], [camera angle], [light and contact shadow], [paper/plastic/metal detail], [limited palette], [crop and blank text-safe areas], [ratio], no readable lettering or logos.

Describe the asset, not an entire finished webpage. Name the subject, intended page role, viewpoint, light, materials, palette, negative space, crop, and exclusions. Request one image per role. HTML owns headlines, body text, prices, navigation, labels, and controls.

## Complete sample prompts

These prompts are for fictional projects and concept assets. They are ready to adapt to a real brief; no output files have been generated by this documentation change.

### Analog hero / 3:2

> Overhead photographic composition of a dark teal cutting mat, two blank instant-photo frames, a small orange pencil, a pressed leaf, and one photograph of a coastal path, warm paper tabletop, realistic paper fibers and subtle edge wear, gentle light from upper left with consistent contact shadows, objects grouped toward the right with calm space on the left, personal analog desk mood, 3:2 landscape, no words, no dates, no brand marks, no website controls.

### Collection cover / 1:1

> Square artwork concept of a blank record sleeve partly revealing a dark vinyl disc, a small unprinted cream label and a cropped photographic view of a rainy city window, restrained teal and warm gray palette, softly worn paper corners, overhead view, consistent light from upper left, 1:1 square, no typography, no artist names, no logos, no promotional stickers.

### Device variant / 4:5

> Studio 3D render of an unbranded early-1990s-inspired handheld electronic organizer, chunky matte teal plastic housing, small amber key accents and a blank gray-green screen, front three-quarter view, physically plausible seams and buttons, one softbox above left, subtle contact shadow, clean isolated background, 4:5 portrait with safe margin around the whole object, no letters, no logos, no screen interface, no chrome futurism.

## Negative direction

Avoid: A random pile of era cues, heavy sepia, pervasive scratches, illegible pixel text, neon synthwave backgrounds without a matching brief, fake brands, fake application UI, and simulated buttons that look clickable inside an image.

Do not merely append “premium,” “beautiful,” or “award-winning.” Replace those words with visible decisions about scale, lighting, material, and composition. If the subject is wrong, revise the subject before adding visual effects.

## Crop, theme, and series consistency

Allow 10% margin around paper edges and objects. For phones, use a fresh compact arrangement with fewer objects instead of scaling down the desktop collage. Export transparent cutouts only when the edge and shadow work on both semantic backgrounds.

Do not insert the device prompt into an analog desk series by default. If device retro is chosen, regenerate the hero, details, and supporting assets with the same device camera, light, and material rules.

Lock the light direction, lens/viewpoint family, color treatment, and material language for a series. Vary the distance and subject detail, not the entire art direction. Review both the full-size image and a small catalogue thumbnail. Do not invert photography for dark mode; use deliberate crops or compatible transparent edges. Check cutouts on both the light and dark surfaces for halos and lost shadows.

## Handoff to JabKit

Choose each image's role and aspect ratio before generating. Keep the original high-quality output, then create web-sized derivatives with explicit width and height and appropriate responsive sources. Use a meaningful alt description for informative imagery and empty alt for purely decorative assets. Keep a text fallback when media fails.

Store component media locally under the hosted `/assets/...` convention. Follow [preview and asset documentation](../../docs/previews.md); the current vendor command handles supported remote sources and is not a general AI-image importer. Record provenance in the format supported by the repository rather than inventing a remote URL for a generated file. Do not hotlink reference-site assets in components or mocks.

## Art-direction acceptance

The image must serve its section, preserve a recognizable subject at mobile size, leave the required quiet area, and belong to the same series as its neighbors. Reject attractive images with implausible materials, broken object geometry, unintentional text, inconsistent shadows, or a crop that obstructs the action. The page should still communicate the offer if the image is unavailable.
